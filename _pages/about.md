    <p class="kh-kicker">About Kedma</p>
    <h1>I have spent my career moving between making things, understanding people, and building evidence.</h1>

    <p>
      My route into AI was not a straight line—and that is part of what I bring
      to the work. I started in product development, built and ran a direct-to-consumer
      business, moved into market insights and digital marketing, and then into
      computational research and applied AI.
    </p>

    <p>
      Today I work at the intersection of <strong>AI, decision intelligence,
      digital behavior, and human impact</strong>. I am especially interested in
      what unstructured signals—text, image, audio, and video—can reveal before
      changes become visible in surveys, CRM, sales, or other structured data.
    </p>

<section class="kh-about-principles">
  <p class="kh-kicker">What connects my work</p>
  <h2>Three principles I keep coming back to.</h2>

  <div class="kh-principle-grid">
    <article>
      <span>01</span>
      <h3>Unstructured data can be an early signal</h3>
      <p>People speak, search, post, watch, react, and create before those changes appear in a dashboard. I use multimodal digital behavior to make those shifts visible earlier.</p>
    </article>

    <article>
      <span>02</span>
      <h3>AI output is not ground truth</h3>
      <p>Speed matters, but so do validation and accountability. My workflows use theory, human gold standards, model comparison, and audit layers rather than treating automated classification as truth.</p>
    </article>

    <article>
      <span>03</span>
      <h3>Complexity should become usable</h3>
      <p>A method is only valuable if people can understand what it means for a decision. That is why research, public tools, executive briefings, and teaching belong in the same portfolio.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>
