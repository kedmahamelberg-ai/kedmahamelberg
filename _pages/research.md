<section id="programme-1" class="kh-research-program">
  <div class="kh-research-program__number">01</div>

  <div>
    <p class="kh-kicker">AI, Communication & Digital Behavior</p>
    <h2>How do organizations and people communicate when the stakes are high?</h2>

    <p>
      This programme treats unstructured digital behavior—text, image, audio,
      and video—as an early signal of how narratives, preferences, and public
      responses are changing. It studies brands, CEOs, institutions, and
      consumers across crises, controversial issues, sustainability,
      inclusion, and AI-mediated environments.
    </p>

    <div class="kh-research-columns">
      <div>
        <h3>Questions</h3>
        <ul>
          <li>Which forms of communication drive engagement, support, and action?</li>
          <li>How do language, imagery, timing, and messenger identity interact?</li>
          <li>How do digital platforms reshape public narratives around contested issues?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <div>
        <h3>Methods</h3>
        <ul>
          <li>NLP, transformers, multimodal classification, LLM-assisted coding</li>
          <li>30M+ social-media posts across projects plus multimodal text, image, audio, and video data</li>
          <li>Early-signal analysis connecting unstructured behavior to later structured outcomes</li>
          <li>Experiments, panel models, prediction, and causal inference</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="kh-evidence-row">
      <span><strong>30M+</strong> social-media posts across projects</span>
      <span><strong>236k+</strong> war-related tweets in published work</span>
      <span><strong>JPP&amp;M</strong> peer-reviewed publication</span>
    </div>

    <article class="kh-highlight">
      <span>Selected finding</span>
      <p>
        CEO communication can generate stronger public engagement than brand
        communication during geopolitical crisis, with effects depending on
        framing and appeal.
      </p>
      <a
        href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/07439156241230910"
        target="_blank"
        rel="noopener noreferrer"
      >
        Read the paper ↗
      </a>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section id="programme-2" class="kh-research-program">
  <div class="kh-research-program__number">02</div>

  <div>
    <p class="kh-kicker">Human–AI Empowerment & Decision Systems</p>
    <h2>When does AI expand capability—and when does it transfer power away from people?</h2>

    <p>
      This programme examines human–AI relationships through capability,
      creativity, autonomy, control, rights, protection, and public influence.
      It also develops auditable AI workflows for turning noisy digital evidence
      into structured decision signals.
    </p>

    <div class="kh-research-columns">
      <div>
        <h3>Questions</h3>
        <ul>
          <li>When does AI augment people rather than merely automate tasks?</li>
          <li>How do AI systems shift autonomy, ownership, and decision authority?</li>
          <li>How can model output be validated without treating it as ground truth?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <div>
        <h3>Methods</h3>
        <ul>
          <li>LLMs, embeddings, model-auditor architectures, human validation</li>
          <li>Event resolution and multilingual evidence pipelines</li>
          <li>Reliability testing, calibration, governance, and human-in-the-loop review</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>

    <article class="kh-highlight kh-highlight--teal">
      <span>Flagship platform</span>
      <h3>AI Empowerment Observatory</h3>
